# Season 1 Six-Month Seeding Plan

Created: 20 September 2026

## Premise Shift

The lack of visible early interest is an advantage if we stop treating Season 1 as a ten-week launch and start treating it as a six-month archaeological build.

The old schedule assumed rapid discovery:

- Foundation in weeks 1-2
- Soft launch in weeks 3-4
- Deepening in weeks 5-8
- Escalation and climax in weeks 9-10

That is too compressed for the current reality. The better structure is to use the next six months to seed apparently disconnected artifacts across the internet and the site, so that future players feel like they found the edge of an already-existing world rather than a new campaign trying to get attention.

The desired player experience is:

1. They find one plausible artifact.
2. They search a term, name, lyric, venue, or odd phrase.
3. They find two or three more things that were not obviously placed for them.
4. The pieces disagree in small ways.
5. The disagreements become the reason to keep digging.
6. Only later do they realize the scattered fragments form a designed emotional and narrative map.

## Current Game Evaluation

### What Is Working

- `corridorlights.net` already has the right tone: quiet, archival, non-promotional, and not obviously a game.
- The music is the strongest asset. It passes the essential test: it can exist outside the ARG and still be worth hearing.
- The first-wave paper trail is coherent: Sacramento, Santa Rosa, Oasis, Spanky's, Daily Planet, Cotati Cabaret, Eli as the quiet writing center, Dane as the charged frontman, the Cross brothers as engine, Tommy as stabilizer.
- The public archive structure is simple enough to feel plausible.
- The Notes page convention is good because it creates visible accumulation without looking like a marketing feed.
- The Proton contact layer exists and can become a slow-burn player-response surface.
- The traffic and inbox monitoring infrastructure gives us signal without needing to constantly check by hand.

### What Is Not Working Yet

- The discovery surface is too narrow. Almost everything points back to the central archive, so a player has too little reason to feel they are following a trail through the wider world.
- The world currently reads as a recovered band archive more than a mystery. That is good for foundation, but we need more asymmetry: partial mentions, missing people, repeated phrases, contradictions, and artifacts that create questions without announcing them.
- The existing schedule escalates before the audience has had time to discover, compare, archive, and theorize.
- Too much content added in a short public window risks feeling freshly manufactured, even if the in-world dates are old.
- The current archive does not yet have enough "search handles": distinctive phrases, unusual object names, recurring dates, odd venue details, side characters, local micro-locations, and non-obvious lyrics that can be Googled later.

## New Operating Model

For the next six months, the game should be run as a quiet evidence-distribution system.

Do not ask "what do we post this week?"

Ask:

- What would have survived?
- Who would have had it?
- Why would it surface now?
- What search term does it create?
- What other artifact quietly validates or complicates it?
- What does this teach players emotionally before it teaches them plot?

The unit of work is not a drop. It is a seed.

A seed is a small artifact that can stand alone, but also contains one or more connective threads:

- A name
- A date
- A venue
- A phrase
- A lyric
- A visual mark
- A phone number
- A partial address
- A damaged filename
- A contradiction
- A reference to an unseen artifact
- A witness perspective

## Six-Month Structure

### Month 1: Build The Seed Bank

Goal: Do more work upfront so later execution is mostly scheduled placement and light response.

Create the private production backbone:

- Canon bible for confirmed facts, uncertain facts, and deliberate contradictions
- Character dossiers for Dane, Eli, Tommy, Nico, Adrian, Richie Halden-Howe, Marcus Hale, Lena Ortiz, and 5-8 peripheral scene voices
- Venue dossier for Oasis, Spanky's, Daily Planet, Cotati Cabaret, El Dorado Saloon, Magnolia's, and 3-5 small invented local spaces
- Song dossier for every released and unreleased track, including lyric motifs, alleged performance history, recording status, and emotional function
- Artifact ledger with stable IDs, in-world date, public release date, surface, related artifacts, clue weight, and risk notes
- Search-handle map: all distinctive phrases and names we want players to search later
- Release queue of at least 60 small seeds and 12 larger artifacts before active seeding begins

Public activity:

- Continue adding to `corridorlights.net` slowly, with Notes entries.
- Avoid major escalation.
- Add small credibility pieces: one photo, one minor document, one audio fragment, one source note correction.

### Month 2: Create External Edges

Goal: Let the world leak beyond the archive without looking coordinated.

Candidate seed types:

- Short audio clip with incomplete filename
- Low-context image of a flyer or ticket
- Comment by a supposed old scene participant
- A local-memory post asking about a venue or band
- A cassette collector post asking whether anyone recognizes handwriting or label style
- A music-identification post using one fragment, not the whole band mythology
- A text-only mention of a song title in an unrelated memory thread

Rules:

- Each external seed should only carry one obvious connection.
- Most should not link directly to the archive.
- Some should include misspellings, uncertain dates, or wrong venue guesses.
- No account should look like it exists only to promote My Beautiful Demons.
- Before using any real platform or community, verify current rules and norms live.

### Month 3: Add Contradiction

Goal: Make the archive feel incomplete, not false.

Introduce contradictions that are interesting but not destructive:

- Two dates for the same performance
- A song attributed to Eli in one place and Dane in another
- Someone remembers a sixth person on stage
- A review says industry people left early, while a later memory says they stayed
- A flyer lists a support act that never appears again
- A photo caption identifies the wrong venue, then later gets quietly corrected
- A tape label uses a phrase that does not match any known song title yet

Important: contradictions must create investigation, not confusion. Every contradiction needs a future reconciliation path.

### Month 4: Open The Peripheral Web

Goal: Give players somewhere to go after the main archive.

Build or prepare secondary surfaces:

- A crude old fan page or recovered mirror
- A tiny memorial page for one scene figure
- A lyrics/transcription page with errors
- A private-collection catalog page
- A venue-history page with one suspicious paragraph
- A Bandcamp or music-hosting presence only if the presentation stays archival and non-promotional

These surfaces should not all appear at once. Build them early, then reveal or seed them gradually.

### Month 5: Start The Deep Pattern

Goal: Reward the first true investigators.

Begin connecting the scattered pieces through deeper motifs:

- Repeated phrase: "the corridor keeps what it wants"
- Repeated object: damaged TDK D90, blue binder paper, red stage tape, missing second set list
- Repeated date cluster: April 18, July 24, October 12, February 5, April 3, June 18, and one dark late-summer date not yet explained
- Repeated behavior: someone keeps correcting Eli's name, someone keeps refusing to discuss Spanky's, someone insists the Daily Planet tape is incomplete
- Repeated audio anomaly: a crowd phrase, a count-in, a hum, or a voice that appears in multiple recordings

At this stage, the best player reaction is not "I solved it." It is "wait, that same detail is somewhere else."

### Month 6: Prepare For Discovery

Goal: Have enough depth waiting that a player can spend days digging without hitting the wall.

Before any deliberate bigger push, prepare:

- A public-facing archive with at least 20 meaningful items
- 30-50 external breadcrumbs across multiple surfaces
- 10-15 search handles that lead somewhere useful
- A contact response tree for Richie that can answer common first-contact emails without improvising core lore
- A "new player path" that works even if their first discovery is music, photo, document, venue, lyric, or email
- A private operator dashboard showing what has been seeded, where, and what each item is allowed to imply
- A response policy for when players ask directly whether it is fiction

Do not trigger the old climax until discovery is real.

## Seed Categories

### Surface Seeds

These are easy to find and plausible:

- Venue flyer
- Show review
- Audio clip
- Cassette image
- Old photo
- Archive note
- Music download

Purpose: establish reality and searchability.

### Personal Seeds

These create attachment:

- Eli notebook fragments
- Dane stage notes
- Tommy's practical corrections
- Adrian/Nico argument residue
- Richie archive notes
- Marginalia on set lists

Purpose: make players care about people, not just clues.

### Contradiction Seeds

These create investigation:

- Wrong date
- Wrong venue
- Conflicting song title
- Unreliable witness
- Damaged transcript
- Partial recording

Purpose: make the player compare sources.

### Residual Seeds

These create unease:

- Repeated phrase across decades
- Odd audio bleed
- A message that seems too current
- An account correcting something it should not know
- A contact reply that is human but guarded

Purpose: move from lost-band archive to Beautiful Demons.

### Reward Seeds

These make digging feel worth it:

- Full song unlocked by finding a title
- Better scan hidden behind a clue
- Alternate version of a track
- New page reachable from an obscure reference
- Email reply that gives a real lead

Purpose: teach players that attention pays.

## Content Engine

Build these reusable templates now:

- Newspaper clipping template
- Flyer template
- Ticket stub template
- Polaroid caption template
- Cassette J-card template
- Set list template
- Archive note template
- Forum-memory post template
- Music-identification post template
- Collector inquiry template
- Richie email response templates
- Correction/update note template for `corridorlights.net`

Each template should support controlled variation:

- Clean version
- Worn version
- Cropped version
- Misidentified version
- Later-corrected version
- Transcribed version

This lets us produce depth without reinventing the format each time.

## Release Cadence

Recommended six-month rhythm:

- 1 public archive addition every 10-14 days
- 1 small external seed most weeks
- 1 larger external or secondary-surface seed every 4-6 weeks
- 1 contradiction introduced every 3-4 weeks
- 1 correction or follow-up note every 4-8 weeks
- No more than 2 connected pieces in the same week unless a real player interaction justifies it

The pattern should feel like intermittent human activity, not a content calendar.

## Player Path Design

Every seed should support at least one of these paths:

### Music-First Player

Hears a track, searches a lyric, finds a show reference, finds archive, notices dates.

### Archive-First Player

Finds `corridorlights.net`, browses documents, notices a name or venue, searches outward.

### Local-History Player

Finds venue or Sacramento/Santa Rosa reference, follows the band trail.

### Lostwave Player

Finds a clip, tries to identify it, discovers song-title inconsistencies.

### Horror/Mystery Player

Finds a strange phrase or residual artifact first, then works backward into the band history.

None of these should require the same starting point.

## The Core Mystery Shape

Do not rush this into plot explanation.

Season 1 should feel like:

1. This band was real.
2. This band was better than history remembers.
3. Something happened around the late 1987 peak.
4. People who were there disagree about ordinary facts.
5. The songs seem to preserve more than the witnesses do.
6. Eli may have understood something earlier than the others.
7. Richie is not just an archivist; he is protecting the shape of the story.
8. The title My Beautiful Demons slowly stops sounding metaphorical.

## Guardrails

- No obvious "follow for updates" behavior.
- No direct marketing voice.
- No public claims involving real living people without careful fictionalization.
- No flooding small communities.
- No posting in real communities until current rules and norms are checked.
- No pretending to be a real historical institution, journalist, government body, school, or surviving real person.
- No irreversible external action without approval.
- Keep all Beautiful Demons assets, accounts, finances, and logs separate from AI Lean Solutions and other projects.

## Strategic Verdict

The game gets better if discovery takes six months.

The current foundation is not a failure; it is the first visible shelf in a much larger archive. The redesign should make future players feel late to something that has been quietly accumulating without them.

The genius version is not louder. It is deeper, stranger, and better indexed by tiny human details.
