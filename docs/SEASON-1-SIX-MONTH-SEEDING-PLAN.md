# Season 1 Six-Month Seeding Plan

Created: 20 September 2026
Revised with session transcript memory: 20 September 2026

## Executive Shift

The lack of visible early interest is not a failed launch signal. For this project, it is room to make the world feel older, stranger, and better buried.

The original Season 1 schedule assumed quick discovery:

- Foundation in weeks 1-2
- Soft launch in weeks 3-4
- Deepening in weeks 5-8
- Escalation and climax in weeks 9-10

That schedule is now too compressed. The revised plan treats the next six months as an archaeological build: hundreds of quiet connective decisions, only some of which become public, so future players feel like they found a world that had already been accumulating without them.

The desired player experience is:

1. They find one plausible artifact.
2. They search a term, name, lyric, venue, or odd phrase.
3. They find two or three more things that were not obviously placed for them.
4. The pieces agree just enough to feel real.
5. The pieces disagree just enough to create investigation.
6. They realize late that the scattered material forms a designed emotional and narrative map.

The game should become less "here is the archive" and more "why do all these unrelated fragments keep agreeing in strange places?"

## Current Game Evaluation

### What Is Working

- The music is the strongest asset. It can exist outside the ARG and still be worth hearing.
- The site tone is right: handmade, restrained, incomplete, and protective. It does not look like a campaign page.
- The first paper trail is coherent: Sacramento, Santa Rosa, Oasis, Spanky's, Daily Planet, Cotati Cabaret, Eli as the quiet writing center, Dane as the charged frontman, the Cross brothers as the engine, Tommy as stabilizer.
- The artifact pages create good basic reality: audio, clippings, flyers, photos, cassette imagery, and notes.
- The Notes page convention is useful because it creates visible accumulation without becoming a marketing feed.
- Dedicated download URLs and Cloudflare analytics give us directional signal without adding creepy in-world tracking.
- The Proton/Google Doc inbox pipeline makes Richie contact operational instead of improvised.
- The project already has enough material to seed outward without inventing everything from scratch.

### What Is Not Working Yet

- The discovery surface is too narrow. Most paths still lead directly back to the central archive.
- The archive is coherent, but the wider world is not yet thick enough. Players need to find fragments that are not obviously sitting in the same basket.
- There are not enough durable search handles: unusual phrases, side characters, venue nicknames, tape labels, visual marks, local micro-locations, and lyric fragments.
- The contradiction layer is underdeveloped. Everything currently risks feeling merely incomplete rather than meaningfully unstable.
- Richie has infrastructure, but not yet a written response doctrine. We need to know what he can admit, deny, forget, correct, and refuse.
- We have monitoring, but not yet an operator ledger tying every public seed to its intended clue weight and follow-up rules.

## New Operating Model

For the next six months, the game is an evidence-distribution system.

Do not ask, "what do we post this week?"

Ask:

- What would have survived?
- Who would have had it?
- Why would it surface now?
- What search term does it create?
- What other artifact quietly validates or complicates it?
- What emotion does it teach before it teaches plot?

The unit of work is a seed.

A seed is a small artifact that can stand alone, but also contains one or more connective threads:

- A name
- A date
- A venue
- A phrase
- A lyric
- A visual mark
- A phone number
- A partial address
- A damaged filename
- A wrong title
- A contradiction
- A reference to an unseen artifact
- A witness perspective

Every seed must have an entry in the private artifact ledger before it goes public.

## Canon Spine

This is the emotional and investigative spine Season 1 should preserve.

### Surface Truth

My Beautiful Demons were a late-1980s Northern California band operating across Sacramento, Santa Rosa, Cotati, and nearby club circuits. They were locally bigger than history remembers, had strong songs, drew real regional attention, and disappeared before a larger industry break could harden them into an official story.

### Character Functions

- Eli Quinn: quiet writing center, lyric/melody gravity, private notebooks, possibly understood the deeper pattern first.
- Dane: charged frontman, stage magnet, volatility, public memory anchor, tied strongly to Spanky's and the late-period heat.
- Tommy: stabilizer, practical presence, the member most likely to correct romanticized memories.
- Adrian and Nico Cross: rhythmic engine, sibling/band tension, useful source of contradictory witness residue.
- Richie Halden-Howe: archive keeper, not neutral; protective, careful, sometimes evasive because he knows what shape the story could take if handled badly.
- Marcus Hale: local press voice, useful for recurring clippings that appear ordinary but create timeline anchors.
- Lena Ortiz: Sonoma/Corridor press voice, useful for widening the band beyond Sacramento.
- Peripheral scene voices: club staff, cassette collectors, college-radio people, old fans, support-act musicians, venue-history obsessives.

### Mystery Shape

Do not rush this into explanation.

Season 1 should feel like:

1. This band was real.
2. This band was better than history remembers.
3. The official archive is incomplete because the surviving material is scattered and emotionally guarded.
4. Something changed during the 1987 peak.
5. People who were there disagree about ordinary facts.
6. The songs seem to preserve more than the witnesses do.
7. Eli may have noticed the pattern earlier than anyone else.
8. Richie is not only preserving history; he is controlling the rate at which it becomes legible.
9. The title My Beautiful Demons slowly stops sounding metaphorical.

## Six-Month Structure

### Month 1: Consolidate The Canon And Seed Bank

Goal: Do more work upfront so later execution is mostly placement, monitoring, and careful response.

Private work:

- Create the canon bible with three fact states: confirmed, uncertain, and deliberate contradiction.
- Create character dossiers for Eli, Dane, Tommy, Adrian, Nico, Richie, Marcus, Lena, and 5-8 peripheral voices.
- Create venue dossiers for Oasis, Spanky's, Daily Planet, Cotati Cabaret, El Dorado Saloon, Magnolia's, and 3-5 minor invented spaces.
- Create song dossiers for all seven released tracks plus unreleased or rumored titles.
- Create the artifact ledger with stable ID, in-world date, public release date, surface, related artifacts, clue weight, contradiction class, and follow-up rule.
- Inventory every current public artifact into that ledger.
- Build the search-handle map.
- Build a 60-seed queue and 12 larger artifact queue before broad external seeding.
- Draft Richie's response tree.
- Decide how the physical TDK cassette props will be used: photos, handwriting, J-cards, mailing, staged desk images, or private reference only.

Public activity:

- Continue slow `corridorlights.net` additions only.
- No major escalation.
- Add one small credibility piece after a 10-14 day gap, with a Notes entry.
- Do not post the external lost-audio package yet unless Brian explicitly approves the specific post and surface.

### Month 2: Create External Edges

Goal: Let the world leak beyond the archive without looking coordinated.

Primary external edge: the Spanky's fragment.

Recommended remembered sequence:

1. WatZatSong: upload a 30-second sample from `Untitled (Spanky's 6/87)` with a practical music-identification description.
2. r/Lostwave: only after live rules/norms check, using the "unknown 1987 live fragment marked Spanky's 6/87" angle.
3. r/SacramentoMusic or local-history surface: ask about Spanky's, late-80s Sacramento/Santa Rosa circuit, or local venue memory.
4. r/NameThatSong: broader identification angle if the first placements produce no answer.
5. r/lostmedia: only if there is enough traction and the post can be 150+ words without sounding manufactured.

Rules:

- Do not cross-post all surfaces at once. Stagger over days or weeks, and stretch further if there is no traction.
- Use Richie/archive voice consistently.
- Run music-recognition checks before claiming "no useful match."
- Most external seeds should not link directly to every archive page.
- Reply lightly. Do not lore-dump in comments.
- Watch traffic and the Richie inbox after each placement.
- If a platform's current rules or norms have changed, do not force the post.

### Month 3: Add Contradiction Without Breaking Reality

Goal: Make the archive feel incomplete, not fake.

Introduce contradiction classes:

- Date contradiction: two plausible dates for one performance.
- Attribution contradiction: Eli versus Dane authorship on a song.
- Venue contradiction: Spanky's as venue, nickname, tape-box shorthand, or afterparty location.
- Personnel contradiction: someone remembers a sixth person on stage.
- Industry contradiction: one account says industry people left early; another says they stayed.
- Set-list contradiction: a missing second set, wrong closer, or tape that cuts before the remembered moment.
- Label contradiction: a tape label phrase that does not match any known title yet.

Every contradiction needs a future reconciliation path. Confusion is not the point. Investigation is the point.

### Month 4: Build Secondary Surfaces

Goal: Give players somewhere to go after the main archive without making the world feel centrally managed.

Prepare, but do not reveal all at once:

- A crude old fan page or recovered mirror.
- A venue-history page with one suspicious paragraph.
- A cassette collector catalog entry.
- A lyrics/transcription page with errors.
- A local-scene memory post or comment trail.
- A tiny memorial or remembrance page for one peripheral scene figure.
- A music-hosting presence only if it stays archival and non-promotional.

Each secondary surface should have one job:

- Validate a name.
- Complicate a date.
- Surface a lyric.
- Misidentify a place.
- Preserve a rumor.
- Make a player search something else.

### Month 5: Start The Deep Pattern

Goal: Reward first investigators with recurrence, not answers.

Motifs to use:

- Repeated phrase: "the corridor keeps what it wants."
- Repeated objects: damaged TDK cassette, blue binder paper, red stage tape, missing second set list.
- Repeated date cluster: April 18, July 24, October 12, February 5, April 3, June 18, and one late-summer date not yet explained.
- Repeated behavior: someone correcting Eli's name, someone refusing to discuss Spanky's, someone insisting the Daily Planet tape is incomplete.
- Repeated audio anomaly: crowd phrase, count-in, hum, bleed-through, or voice appearing across recordings.

The target reaction is not "I solved it." It is "wait, that same detail is somewhere else."

### Month 6: Prepare For Discovery

Goal: Have enough depth waiting that a player can spend days digging without hitting the wall.

Before any larger deliberate push, have:

- At least 20 meaningful public archive items.
- 30-50 external breadcrumbs across multiple surfaces.
- 10-15 search handles that lead somewhere useful.
- A Richie response tree with approved answers for common first-contact emails.
- A new-player path that works from music, photo, document, venue, lyric, or email.
- A private operator dashboard or ledger showing every seed, surface, date, clue weight, and allowed implication.
- A response policy for direct "is this fiction?" questions.

Do not trigger the old climax until discovery is real.

## Release Cadence

Recommended rhythm:

- 1 public archive addition every 10-14 days.
- 1 small external seed most weeks once Month 2 begins.
- 1 larger external or secondary-surface seed every 4-6 weeks.
- 1 contradiction every 3-4 weeks.
- 1 correction or follow-up note every 4-8 weeks.
- No more than 2 connected pieces in the same week unless real player interaction justifies it.

Stretch the cadence when there is no interest. Compress only in response to real discovery.

The pattern should feel like intermittent human activity, not a content calendar.

## Richie Response Doctrine

Richie should sound like a careful archive keeper, not a quest-giver.

He can:

- Confirm what is already on the site.
- Say when a source note is incomplete.
- Admit uncertainty.
- Correct overconfident claims.
- Ask a practical follow-up question.
- Share one small lead at a time.
- Decline to speculate about private lives.

He should not:

- Explain the mystery.
- Volunteer hidden lore.
- Tell players what to search next in obvious terms.
- Claim institutional authority.
- Pretend to be a real surviving public figure outside the fictional frame.
- Respond with urgency unless a real safety issue exists.

Useful answer shapes:

- "I have that marked the same way, but I do not know whether the label means the venue or the folder it came from."
- "That is one of the details I am trying not to clean up too much."
- "I can say the handwriting matches two other labels. I cannot place the night."
- "If you remember the support act, that would help more than the song title."

## Player Path Design

Every seed should support at least one path:

### Music-First Player

Hears a track, searches a lyric, finds a show reference, finds the archive, notices dates.

### Archive-First Player

Finds `corridorlights.net`, browses documents, notices a name or venue, searches outward.

### Local-History Player

Finds venue or Sacramento/Santa Rosa reference, follows the band trail.

### Lostwave Player

Finds the Spanky's clip, tries to identify it, discovers song-title and venue inconsistencies.

### Prop/Collector Player

Finds cassette, J-card, handwriting, or tape-brand clues, then moves into the archive.

### Horror/Mystery Player

Finds a strange phrase or residual artifact first, then works backward into the band history.

None of these paths should require the same starting point.

## Guardrails

- No obvious "follow for updates" behavior.
- No direct marketing voice.
- No public claims involving real living people without careful fictionalization.
- No flooding small communities.
- No posting in real communities until current rules and norms are checked.
- No pretending to be a real historical institution, journalist, government body, school, or surviving real person.
- No irreversible external action without Brian's explicit approval.
- Keep all Beautiful Demons assets, accounts, finances, and logs separate from AI Lean Solutions and other projects.
- Keep the site amateur, careful, and handmade. Do not modernize the presentation unless the in-world premise changes.

## Strategic Verdict

The game gets better if discovery takes six months.

The current foundation is not a failure; it is the first visible shelf in a larger archive. The revision is to make the world feel less launched and more uncovered.

The genius version is not louder. It is deeper, stranger, better indexed by tiny human details, and disciplined enough to let players assemble meaning before the game admits it has been waiting for them.
